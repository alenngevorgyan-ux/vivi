# VIVI — content bible v1

## Editorial north star

“Oh, what would I do?” A strong Vivi post presents a plausible situation with personal stakes, conflicting loyalties, a physical environment, one escalating modifier, and no simple answer. It is understandable in under ten seconds, playable in 2–5 minutes, and safe to share without spoiling the reveal.

## Ten content pillars

1. Relationship betrayal: trust, hidden messages, divided loyalty.
2. Creepy real life: doors, cameras, repeated calls, surveillance clues. Stay plausible.
3. Social disaster: leaks, lies, public embarrassment, group chat fractures.
4. Work and power: stolen credit, authority, whistleblowing, career risk.
5. Money: debt, cash offers, scams, obligations.
6. Family secrets: documents, relatives, identity, old lies.
7. Strange human moments: uncanny coincidences and disputed shared memories.
8. Moral dilemmas: truth, protection, intervention, self-preservation.
9. Romance and intimacy: adult boundaries, confession, goodbye, jealousy.
10. Life turning points: quitting, moving, accepting or leaving.

## Hook rules

Use first-person human language, one concrete object, one sharp uncertainty. Feed cards expose setup and stakes only. Do not reveal the suspect, explanation, author action, ending, crowd preference, or moral framing in title, art, alt text, metadata or share copy. Art should show the location and meaningful object at a distance. Recheck the spoiler boundary whenever a scene thumbnail changes.

## Scenario acceptance rubric

Score each criterion 0–2: instant clarity; curiosity; personal stakes; contested answer; useful space; contextual modifier; commit before reveal; 2–5 minute runtime; shareable hook; interesting comparison; “happened to me too” potential. Aim for 17/22 or higher. Reject passive “walk and read memories” submissions until a consequential act is found. Avoid repetitive phone-first mechanics by rotating arrival, distance, authority, public visibility, object evidence, dialogue and silence.

## Content safety and craft

Do not manufacture a false “true story” claim. Give authors a way to anonymize places and people, remove identifying details, and preview the reveal. Tension may be uncomfortable, but avoid glamorizing stalking, coercion or unsafe confrontation. “Call for help,” “leave,” and “wait” can be valid paths. Depict consequences honestly, without calling one path morally correct.
