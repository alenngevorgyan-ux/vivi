# Vivi Visual Language V3: "The Remembered Room"

Art-direction specification · 2026-10-03 · branch `planning/vivi-experience-v3` · no production code changed.

Scope: the **playable human stories inside Vivi** (scenes, figures, light, camera, marks, reveal). Not the website shell. Architecture comes from the [V3 master plan](VIVI_EXPERIENCE_V3_MASTER_PLAN.md); this document replaces its section L ("painted paper diorama") with a more specific decision, explained in §3.

**Honest status.** The boards are *programmatic proofs*: world plates, figures, light and texture are generated from a few hundred lines of SVG/filters, then typeset. They prove the **composition, the material system, the light logic, the mark language, the typography and the reveal choreography**. They do **not** prove final illustration quality. Specific known weaknesses:

* Figures are stiff and several poses (notice / hesitate / avoid) are too similar at this fidelity; knees and torsos are toy-like. Body language will only work with an illustrator-made rig (§9). Do not treat the figure on the boards as the target.
* The "paint" is displaced vector shapes plus baked-style noise. It reads as gouache from a distance and as vector up close.
* Direction A and B on `board-00` are quick approximations of those styles, enough to compare *reading*, not to judge them at their best.
* Nothing has been tested on a device, with sound, or with Armenian text. The Newsreader/Inter/Plex pair is rendered; Noto Serif Armenian is installed locally but no Armenian specimen was reviewed.
* Story texts on the boards are the fictional editorial QA fixtures (apartment) or invented for the boards (office/lift/envelope; labelled fictional).

## 1. Audit of current Vivi

Evidence: `reports/experience-v2/02, 08, 09, 10, 11` screenshots; `SceneArt.tsx` (763 lines), `CharacterFigure.tsx` (517), `characters.ts`, `world/templates/index.ts`, `cameraLanguage.ts`, `CanonicalViviEngine.tsx`, `index.css`.

### Why it reads as procedural SVG

1. **One horizon, one darkness.** Every world meets its floor at `FLOOR_Y = 284` and every scene gets the same stack: base darkness, soft-light tint, radial vignette (`.vivi-grade-*`), plus per-scene dark rect. In the three screenshots (apartment, office, hallway) the room differs, the *photograph of the room* does not: same blue-black, same centered vignette, same floor-plane fall-off. Nothing is composed asymmetrically.
2. **Frontal planes, no weight.** Backdrops are stacks of `<rect>`s (window, door, wall) with a floor polygon. There are no cast shadows between objects, no wall/floor contact darkening, no material change. A sofa and a table have the same surface.
3. **Seven of ten worlds are `Generic*`** (`SceneArt.tsx:511–660`), parameterised by palette. Only apartment, hallway and office are bespoke. The "world kit" is a colour swap.
4. **Palette-swap people.** `characters.ts` differs by skin, hair, clothing, trouser, build and hair shape. In the office screenshot five people share one body and one pose family at the same size. Faces are two dots. Emotion has nowhere to live.
5. **Procedural texture.** `feTurbulence` at 0.055 opacity overlay, per layer, at runtime. It is uniform and scale-independent, which is the signature of a filter, not a surface.
6. **Interaction looks like a game.** `.vivi-hotspot-ring` is a 14px cream disc with glow and a 2.8s breathing animation; the selected state is a rust halo. This is the universal "interactable" signifier. Several dots appear at once (hallway: five), so the scene reads as a level with pickups.
7. **The story lives outside the picture.** The most important human information (the message, the stakes) is a card below the stage. On mobile (screenshot 11) half the viewport is blank paper under the card.
8. **Anonymous specifics.** The slide in the office is grey bars; the phone in the apartment is a bright dot; QA already flagged invented art details (clock time, laptop, intercom). Nothing about the world is *this person's*.
9. **Reveal is a different product.** `RevealView` is a long white editorial card page with a textarea and CTA (screenshot 08). The world is gone; there is no transformation.

### Keep

* Four-layer contract (backdrop / midground / figures depth-sorted / foreground occluder / light) and the idea that `SceneState` drives art, never a story id.
* **Subtractive lighting**: paint darkness, carve light out. Practicals with semantic roles (lamp, door seam, screen).
* 1000×600 world coordinates, percent positions, walkable polygons, slot IDs, the semantic staging presets, depth-sort by y, figure size derived from stage width.
* Eleven camera presets and the no-overshoot easings; "frame clamped to the world".
* Honesty rules from V2/V3 plan (no invented reactions, held frame ≠ real silence, boundary before reveal).
* Paper / ink / rust shell tokens, Newsreader + Inter + IBM Plex Mono.

### Abandon

* A universal horizon at 47%; every world drawn as a full rectangular bleed with identical vignette.
* The glowing dot as the discovery layer; pulsing anything; "talk bubble" chips on dark.
* People as `CharacterFigure` palette swaps; two-dot faces.
* Runtime SVG turbulence as the surface.
* Story context in a card under the world; mobile 4:3 stage with controls below.
* Reveal as a page that replaces the world.
* Dark blue-grey as the default "serious" colour.

## 2. Three directions

All three share the master plan's architecture (plates, rigs, marks above DOM, no model in playback). They differ in **material, perspective and what the frame edge means**. See `board-00-directions.jpg`.

### A. Cut-paper diorama (the plan's earlier favourite)

* **World:** frontal shadow-box; back / middle / front cut layers with real drop shadows; large flat colour areas; heavy negative space. Hero prop as a raised cut-out.
* **Characters:** paper dolls, simplified proportions, white cut edge, no faces or a few dots; silhouette readable but toy-scale.
* **Material:** card stock, glue edges, soft inter-layer shadows. Layered raster, parallax-friendly.
* **Light:** ambient plus soft shadow; practicals are tinted paper, not sources.
* **Color:** lifted, saturated, friendly.
* **Camera:** parallax pushes, slow lateral slides; inserts are bigger cut-outs.
* **Motion:** stepped, puppet-like; walking is sliding with a sway.
* **Interaction:** objects "stand proud" of their layer (a lifted edge / shadow). Commitment would be a paper tab.
* **Text:** typewriter labels on card.
* **Reveal:** curtain of paper lifts.
* **Verdict:** beautiful and cheap to parallax, but reads as a *miniature you look down on*: charming, crafted, distant. Pastel seams of the kit show. Children's-book risk. Weakest at "I entered somebody's memory".

### B. Ink planes (graphic-novel panels)

* **World:** orthographic, few huge planes, hard silhouettes, panel borders and gutters as the camera.
* **Characters:** black pictogram silhouettes, no interior, gesture by contour.
* **Material:** two or three flat inks plus paper, optional halftone. Vector, tiny files.
* **Light:** binary; light is a shape.
* **Color:** ink black, one accent, paper.
* **Camera:** cuts between panels; montage grammar.
* **Motion:** held poses, panel-to-panel.
* **Interaction:** an object is "inked" when relevant. Commitment is a thick border.
* **Text:** caption boxes, speech-free.
* **Reveal:** the panel becomes a caption.
* **Verdict:** distinctive and the cheapest runtime, but practical light, material and the sense of *place* disappear, and the body becomes a diagram. It risks looking like a quiz in comic clothing. Good for a *feed thumbnail grammar*, wrong for inhabiting a room.

### C. The Remembered Room (chosen)

* **World:** shallow 3/4 plates with real architecture; a strong asymmetric composition; the **frame is not a rectangle of finished picture**: it is painted where attention rests and left as graphite construction where it does not.
* **Characters:** faceless painted adults, slightly long-limbed, head ≈ 1/7 height; face is a lighter plane that turns; hands are the one drawn detail; rim light from the nearest practical.
* **Material:** gouache on visible paper tooth, baked pigment granulation, a deckled painted edge, graphite lines beyond it.
* **Light:** practical pools; the protagonist's coat is always the warmest mass; each location has one warm and one cold source at most.
* **Color:** paper, ink-blue night family, one lamp amber, one rust reserved for decisions and the author.
* **Camera:** holds more than moves; crops of one plate; inserts into prop plates.
* **Motion:** weight, hold and hesitation; on-twos figure timing (12 fps pose changes, eased) while light and camera stay smooth.
* **Interaction:** noticing is *drawn* (graphite loop, italic note); deciding is *inked* (rust bracket + verb).
* **Text:** author voice is large serif on paper; scene lines are typeset in the paper margin, not over the art.
* **Reveal:** the paint lifts back toward paper; the page underneath is the author's.

## 3. Choice: C, the Remembered Room

**Decision: C. No hedge.** It keeps the master plan's materials (painted layered raster, paper shell, editorial type, no model in playback) but replaces the "diorama" idea with one that has a *reason to exist*.

The idea in one sentence: **a Vivi scene is a memory painted only as far as the person remembers it, on the same paper the author later writes on.**

Why it fits:

* **Real human stories.** Memory is incomplete. A frame that is finished at the phone, the hand and the door, and dissolves into graphite elsewhere, is not a style choice but a statement about what is being shown: *this is how one person kept the room*. It is also honest about truth: Vivi does not claim to show the room as it was.
* **Short experiences.** Attention is the editing tool. The finished area can contract to a hand and a phone in 1.4 s: a cut is not needed to say "look here".
* **Social sharing.** A single screenshot is recognisable: a dark painted island with deckled edges on warm paper, graphite lines escaping it, one warm coat, one rust mark. It survives at 120 px (`board-06` strip). The shell and the scene are the same material, so a share card needs no logo.
* **Multi-scene sequences.** Rooms *un-paint and re-paint*; the body and the carried object stay finished through every handoff. Continuity is a rule, not a hope (§11).
* **Mobile.** The deckled edge is permission to *not* fill the rectangle: portrait crops can bleed off three sides and leave the bottom as paper for text, without the "wall strip" problem of squeezing a landscape plate.
* **Cheap, reusable runtime.** Each kit is a *sketch plate* (line) and a *finished plate* (paint) plus a few baked alpha masks. The most expensive part of a normally-lavish illustrated scene (finishing the periphery) is deliberately not made. Seams between kit variants hide in the unfinished edge.
* **Authorial identity.** Nobody else has a world that dissolves into the page.

Why not A or B: A reads as a miniature, distant and toy-like; B loses place and light. Both are *good* directions for other products.

**Where this differs from the master plan §L:** it chooses a *behaviour* (finish follows attention) not just a surface treatment; it moves author text onto the same paper as the world; it retires "warm paper shell vs charcoal worlds" as two things.

## 4. Vivi Visual Language V3: the signature

Recognisable from one screenshot because of seven things together:

1. **The paper ground.** Warm paper (`#ECE4D5`) is always the page. The scene sits on it, never in a card.
2. **The painted island.** Dark, matte gouache, with a **deckled, slightly wet edge** (rough boundary, 20–35 plate-units of irregularity). It bleeds off one or two sides and stops short on the others: asymmetric on purpose.
3. **The unremembered.** Beyond the island the room continues as graphite line (≈0.9 px, 50% opacity, slight wobble): wall lines, floor edge, a table's outline, never filled.
4. **One warm body.** The protagonist's coat (`#C58A3E`) is the warmest, most saturated mass in every frame. Everyone else is cooler and quieter.
5. **Hands and face-plane.** Figures have no features; they have a lit plane that turns and hands that catch light. Anything with a hand has a destination.
6. **Two marks only.** Graphite loops and italic notes for noticing; one rust bracket and a plain verb for deciding.
7. **Silence is typeset.** Scene lines sit in the paper margin in Newsreader italic. Nothing talks over the art. The author's words are the largest type in the product.

### Line, geometry, grain, edges

* **Line:** only graphite (`#4A4A48`) construction lines, 0.9 px at 1×, 50% opacity, with a 3-unit wobble; never outlines around painted shapes.
* **Geometry:** broad planes, one-point or shallow 3/4 depending on kit; no bevels; furniture is a slab with a top, never a rounded icon.
* **Grain:** paper tooth is *baked at constant scale* (does not zoom with the camera); pigment granulation is baked inside the plate. No runtime turbulence filter, no animated noise.
* **Edges:** painted edges are soft-hard; the island's edge is the only place "wetness" is allowed to show.
* **Shadows:** contact shadow under every figure and prop; interior surfaces get ambient darkening at floor, wall and ceiling joins.

### Silence, space, memory

* Negative space is **paper** not "dark nothing". When nothing happens the frame holds; an empty edge is a feature.
* **Memory** is not sepia. A remembered scene is simply a smaller island and more graphite. Flashbacks are labelled in text ("Years earlier"), not tinted.
* **Timing:** holds are long (0.9–1.4 s); camera moves ≤ one per beat; figures animate on twos, light and camera on smooth curves.
* **Never:** red/green guilt split, underlighting, vignette-as-menace, lens flares, sparkles, chromatic aberration, UI glow, faces with expressions.

### Colour

Palette philosophy: **location sets the light, not the mood.** The mood-neutral base is constant; locations are tint families.

| Role | Value | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#ECE4D5` / deep `#E2D8C5` | Always visible; the shell and the reveal |
| Ink (night family) | `#161B26`, walls `#2B2F46`, `#2C3C48`, `#27333D` | Walls are darker than anything lit; never pure black |
| Protagonist coat | `#C58A3E` | Warmest, most saturated mass in frame |
| Lamp amber | `#F0B36A` | Private/human light; domestic and destination |
| Cold practical | `#BFE0E6` | Screens, tubes, lift, bathroom |
| Sodium | `#E08A3C` | Outdoor lamp |
| **Rust** | `#B5523B` | Commitment mark, author entry, "I…" only |
| Graphite | `#4A4A48` | Observation and construction |

Continuity rules: a hero object carries a distinct colour no one else has (envelope cream, phone cold white); the commit/author rust never appears inside the painted world as a *thing* (no red sofa).

## 5. World language

* **Perspective:** per-kit fixed projection owned by the kit manifest (floor polygon, occluders, actor scale curve). Apartment and office: shallow 3/4, eye ≈ 38–45% down; hallway and lift: one-point; street: wide 3/4 with a far destination. No universal horizon.
* **Composition tells a sentence** (master plan §L): "my work is at the front, I am against the wall"; "the phone is reachable, the person is behind a door"; "the lit thing is far and it is not mine".
* **Depth:** background (architecture, distant light), midground (furniture, hero prop), one occluder in front (sofa arm, chair back, door frame). Figures sit between midground and occluder, depth-scaled 0.86 → 1.08.
* **Negative space:** paper margin and dark wall are both holding places. Do not fill a wall with decor that looks inspectable.
* **Hero props:** one high-information object per scene, its own plate, live text (slide, phone, key tag). Two or three supporting details from neutral kit variants; never specific objects (family photo, medication) unless the author supplies them.
* **Environmental detail:** floor texture changes (rug vs boards), baseboard, door hardware, a window's night. Detail density is highest at the hero prop and falls off toward the graphite edge.

World kits at launch: domestic (living + threshold), office (desk + meeting + hallway), lift landing, street, shopfront. See §13.

## 6. Character language

* **Proportions:** adult, head ≈ 1/7 of height, long torso, no oversized heads. Four body families × three clothing variants at launch (`board-07`).
* **No faces.** A lighter face plane that turns with yaw; hair mass; hands lighter than sleeves. Emotion = weight, orientation, hands, distance, timing.
* **Silhouette identity:** families differ in shoulder width, height and coat/jacket shape. The hero is always the only figure with the warm coat.
* **Rim light:** a 2% height sliver of the nearest practical on the lit side; contact shadow always.
* **Carried objects** attach to hand anchors per family (no floating props).

### Reusable body-language vocabulary

21 clips (`board-07`): neutral, notice, listen, hesitate, avoid, turn toward, turn away, reach, stop hand, sit, stand, raise hand, speak, wait, leave, return, freeze, lower gaze, look at another, look at phone, lean-in seated.

Each clip is authored with: anchors (feet, hand-to-prop), lead / hold / release phases, facing coverage (L/R + back where needed), reduced-motion pose, caption string.

**Variation without more clips** comes from four parameters: **order, hold length, distance, and what the body turns toward**.

| Reading | Recipe |
|---|---|
| Decided | notice → reach, no hold, 0.4 s |
| Doubting | reach, stop, hold 1.4 s, half retract, hold |
| Ashamed | lower gaze (0.8 s) → small reach with torso turned away |
| Guarded | stop hand → turn away, distance ×2 |
| Pressured (never imposed on an NPC) | player-only: avoid → freeze |

NPCs get neutral and listen/sit/stand/leave only unless the source or author supplies a gesture (master plan §O). Rig requirement: silhouettes must be distinguishable in a flat black sheet; `notice` and `hesitate` must differ by ≥ 3 body masses (head, torso lean, weight foot), which the proof rig does **not** achieve.

## 7. Camera language

Reuse the eleven presets and easing rules; add a **mark of attention**: the camera and the painted island move *together* (island contracts in the same 1.4 s curve).

| Recipe | Crop (of 1000×625 plate) | Island | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establish | full, ~0–0.6 s settle | wide, bleeds 2 sides | enter; 0.8–1.5 s |
| Attention | 1.4–1.7× toward target | tightens to target + body | cue arrives |
| Insert | 3–4× on a prop plate | edge-hard; margin note | observation |
| Two-shot | midpoint, 1.2× | wide | conversation, distance |
| Over-shoulder | 1.5×, player at 38% | contracted | evidence on a screen |
| Threshold | doorway centred | contracts to the frame of the door | leaving / entering |
| Held | no move | no change | decision, boundary |
| Author still | n/a | gone; page | reveal |

Rules: hold more than move; cut for new information; follow only when travel matters; maximum one automatic emphasis move per beat; frames never reveal plate borders (clamp); mobile uses approved alternate crops, never a squeezed desktop frame; reduced-motion renders the settled crop.

## 8. Light and colour language

* One dominant pool, one secondary, contact shadows. Dark is a **wall value**, never pure black.
* Every location has a stated light role: apartment (amber lamp + cold seam), hallway (two tired practicals + lift), lift (neutral strip), street (one sodium + destination window), office (the screen).
* **Light does not assign guilt.** No red on a "wrong" option, no cool on an "innocent" character, no extra darkness for tension. Pressure is composition, not grade.
* Light changes only on supported events (door opens, phone lights); presentation changes (paint withdraws) never imply the room's lights changed.
* Readability: essential acts are never hidden by dark; focus marks keep independent contrast; high-contrast mode swaps the graphite for ink and lifts wall values.

## 9. Motion language

* Figures: pose-to-pose on twos with eased holds; weight shifts (≈3.4 s breath, ≈5.6 s weight) are the only idle. Walk is distance-driven with ground contact; hesitation is a *hold*, not a loop.
* Light, camera, island: smooth, soft ease, no overshoot (existing tokens `CAMERA_SOFT`, `CAMERA_DRAMATIC`, `REVEAL`).
* Environmental: ventilation, lift hum, window haze stay neutral and never imply arrival or menace.
* Reduced motion: cuts instead of moves; island changes in a single 200 ms fade; poses hold.

## 10. Interaction language (see `board-08`)

Principle: **the world cues; the drawing marks; the verb commits.**

Discovery ladder for one object:

1. **Dormant**: no mark.
2. **Pulled attention**: the world acts (phone lights, head turns, camera drifts 1.4 s, spatial sound). This is the only highlight.
3. **Near**: a graphite loop draws in 240 ms around the object.
4. **Hover / focus / hold (touch 300 ms)**: a two-to-four-word italic note with a leader. Always present as DOM text for screen readers.
5. **Intent chosen**: the note is underlined in graphite. Reversible: Esc, tap elsewhere, walk away.
6. **Commit pending**: the *only* rust-ink mark: a four-corner bracket and a plain verb phrase naming the physical act, with a separate confirm (Enter after release / hold). Exploring cannot reach this state.
7. **Committed**: bracket fades, body performs, island begins to settle.

| | Observation | Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Material | graphite, cream italic | rust ink, flat |
| Geometry | loose loop + leader | closed bracket |
| Type | serif italic, 2–4 words | sans, full verb phrase |
| Timing | 240 ms in, leaves on leave | only after chosen intent |
| Sound | paper brush, no pitch | low wooden tick, once |
| Undo | always | never after confirm; skip fast-forwards only |

People become interactable by **body orientation** (they face you or the target), not by a ring. Doors by an ajar seam of light. Screens by their own glow. "What can I do?" remains a list in the DOM and on touch; targets ≥ 44 CSS px with invisible hit areas; no mandatory hover. Retired: glowing dots, pulse rings, sparkles, floating pills, action grid.

## 11. Multi-scene continuity (see `board-06`)

Sequence: apartment → hallway → elevator → street → destination.

* **The Rule of the Wash.** Rooms un-paint to graphite and re-paint; **the protagonist and the carried object never un-paint**. The eye follows what persists.
* **Recurring palette:** paper, ink, the coat, the hero object's colour, rust (only at decisions). Each room changes its *light*, not its palette family.
* **Character continuity:** same family, same coat, same stride length; the coat can change *only* with a narrative reason and a line of text.
* **Hero-object continuity:** one prop plate, same attachment per body family.
* **Light progression:** warm private lamp → cold fluorescent → neutral lift strip → cold night with one sodium lamp → one warm window ahead. Always one warm thing; at the destination it is the brightest and *not the protagonist's*.
* **Camera grammar:** establish each place with the same 0.8–1.5 s settle; the same height of horizon for the same body; screen direction preserved across the door (hero walks left→right throughout).
* **Transitions:** threshold carry (movement continues: apartment → hallway; street → destination), match cut on the door seam (hallway → lift), cut with the same rider position (lift → street). A location change that doesn't change what the player *knows* gets a carry, not a cut.
* **Sound handoff:** room-tone beds crossfade over the transition (1.0–1.4 s); one-shots belong to events; the destination bed enters before the last step.
* **Material continuity:** paper tooth, island edge treatment, graphite weight and hand style are identical everywhere.
* **Test:** thumbnail strip of the five frames should read as one hand, one coat, one night.

## 12. Reveal language (see `board-09`)

Sequence (≈ 6–7 s before the author's words, all skippable, first author act automatic):

1. **Act finishes.** The body completes exactly the chosen gesture.
2. **Physical silence (0.9 s).** Held frame; grain locks; ambient loops thin. Presentation hold, not a claim that the room fell silent.
3. **World settles (1.6 s).** Paint withdraws from the edges inward; colour leaves walls, then floor, then door. Room lights do not change.
4. **Boundary.** Body and door remain; the body goes flat ink. Line: "That is where your version stops." Sound ducks to room tone and ends.
5. **"You chose…"** One muted line, the player's own act.
6. **"I…"** A single rust word on empty paper (0.8 s hold). Handle and provenance small beneath.
7. **Author's words.** First-person, large serif (28–34 px mobile), unedited; why follows quietly; aftermath on advance only if disclosed.
8. **One continuation.** "Someone else has lived this" / "Tell yours". Note field, counts and similar stories follow, never precede.

Rules: no melodrama (no zoom-in on a face, no music, no flash); no fabricated reaction; reduced motion = dissolve to the page; reveal text is accessible in reading order; network failure keeps the accepted act and offers retry. Camera: none after step 1. Light: none. Texture: grain locks at step 2 and *stays*: the paper in step 6 is the same paper. Typography: the only scale jump in the product happens here.

## 13. Text language

* **Scene lines**: Newsreader italic 17–19 px, in the paper margin below/left of the island; one sentence; reader-paced; stable placement; speaker attribution for quoted speech.
* **Quotes** (a character's actual words) are set in roman quotes with attribution, never italic (italic = the author's narration).
* **Context** is anchored to the frame it concerns, then recedes; the "Context / available actions" control is always reachable.
* **Observation notes** (graphite family): italic, ≤ 4 words.
* **Commit text:** Inter 600, a full verb phrase.
* **Author voice**: Newsreader 400, 28–34 px on mobile, 60-character measure, no animation on text.
* **Multilingual RU / EN / HY:** keep semantic IDs separate from prose; Newsreader has no Armenian, so pair with **Noto Serif Armenian** (candidate, to be specimen-reviewed with `։ ՞ ՜`, long Russian labels, mixed script) and Inter with Noto Sans Armenian. Minimum 16 CSS px body; leave 30% room for RU/HY expansion in margin notes; never rasterise text into plates; no cue timing dependent on font.

## 14. Asset production architecture (offline)

```
art/source/                       (not in the client bundle)
  kits/<kit>/<variant>/
    sketch.psd|svg   line plate: architecture, floor projection, occluders
    finish.psd       finished paint, layered: back / mid / fore / props
    masks/           deckle masks (soft alpha), practical-light masks
    manifest.json    floor polygon, walkable, anchors, depth/scale, portals, safe frames
  props/<prop>/       own plates; live-text areas; hand anchors per family
  characters/<family>/rig.json + part atlases (head/torso/arms/legs/hands, 4 facings) + clothing variants
  clips/<clip>.json   timing, anchors, reduced-motion pose, caption
  light/<kit>/<id>.png   practical masks (screen-blend), exposure recipes
  paper/               tooth + mottle tiles (constant scale)
  sound/               beds, doors, steps, devices (loops + one-shots)
  recipes/{camera,composition,transition,motion}.json
```

Pipeline: composition thumbnail → mobile contact sheet → geometry/anchor validation → illustrator paints finished + sketch → bake masks → export WebP/AVIF at 1×/2× + mobile crop plates → byte/decode budget check → integration snapshot → human review.

* **Generative tools** may be used here only: exploring light, material and non-identifying background candidates; then human selection, repaint/cleanup, geometry alignment, licensing review. Never during authoring or playback.
* **Launch assets:** office (desk, meeting, hallway), domestic (living, threshold), lift landing + car, street, shopfront; 6 hero props (phone, envelope, slide, summary, keys/bag, door/handle); 4 body families × 3 clothing × 4 facings; 21 clips; 8 light masks; 2 paper tiles; ~10 sound beds/one-shots; 3 transition recipes (carry, match cut, wash).
* **Variants** are bounded, reviewed combinations (furniture sets, wall colour, window), never random collage. Evidence-bearing slots (door state, phone, slide) cannot vary.

## 15. Runtime asset architecture (assembly)

Per scene the runtime assembles, never draws:

1. **Paper layer** (constant-scale tooth tile).
2. **Sketch plate** (line), full extent.
3. **Finished plate** composited through a **FinishCompositor**: `canvas 2D`, `destination-in` with one of ~5 baked deckle masks, positioned and scaled to the attention target. Redraw only on mask change (≤ 1.6 s). Fallback: two stacked images with opacity crossfade.
4. **Light masks** (screen blend) tied to scene state (`phoneLit`, `doorState`, `liftLit`).
5. **Figures** as a rig layer (sampled, imperative; no React re-render per frame), always unmasked, with contact shadow and rim colour from the active practical.
6. **Foreground occluder** plate.
7. **DOM**: scene line, margin notes, commit bracket + verb, controls: real text, real focus order.

Manifest-driven: one authoritative spatial manifest per kit drives art anchors, collision, portals, camera safe frames and mobile alternates (master plan §M). Camera is a crop transform of the plate stack; the island mask is moved by the same curve.

Budgets (to validate, from plan §AC): first scene ≤ 1.5 MB plates + 750 KB shared figures; decode ≤ 64 MiB; ≥ 30 fps on the low-end profile; paper tooth and deckle masks are shared across all scenes (cached once).

## 16. Desktop and mobile

* **Desktop (≥ 1024):** stage 16:10, island bleeds top/right, margin below for the scene line; author reveal fills the same frame as page. Hover reveals notes; keyboard focus shows the same note; Enter opens a contextual action, never commits.
* **Mobile portrait (390 × 844):** island occupies the top ~64% and bleeds off top and sides; bottom ~36% is paper carrying the scene line and the "What can I do?" sheet. Approved alternate crops per kit (not the desktop crop squeezed). Touch: tap object → note + intent; hold 300 ms = note only; floor tap = move; no joystick; hit areas ≥ 44 px, invisible.
* **Both:** pause / mute / exit always reachable; reduced-motion and high-contrast variants; scene readable without all narrative text; hero prop and body relation legible at 390 px.

## 20. Risks

| Risk | Signal | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Island reads as vignette/stain, not memory | Players say "why is it blurry?" | Hard-edge deckle, graphite always visible, first-run one-line explanation in the author voice, test A/B with full-bleed |
| Dark wall on paper hurts mobile immersion | Players leave faster | Test full-bleed top/sides on phone; keep paper only at bottom |
| Rig stiffness kills presence | Poses indistinguishable (as in `board-07`) | Illustrator-owned rig, silhouette sheet gate before integration |
| Style costs too much to paint | Kit takes > 3 weeks | Paint finished only the island region at 1×; sketch plate is cheap |
| Graphite reads as unfinished product | "Is it loading?" | Deckle must be intentional; never show line where a hero prop is |
| Painted style imposes guilt/danger | Blind inference test fails | Neutrality review of every frame without text |

Open decisions for the product owner: confirm direction C over the plan's "paper diorama"; license/choose the Armenian serif and sans pair after specimen review; decide whether illustration is done in-house or contracted before Slice V-1 starts.
