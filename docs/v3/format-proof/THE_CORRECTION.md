## A. Complete author source and private account

The following is the final original editorial source, not an attributed real person’s testimony. Span labels are metadata and are not displayed as story prose. All factual amendments supersede the master-plan example only for this fixture.

**S01** — I was on a six-month contract. My team lead, Mira, wrote the recommendation that would decide whether I stayed.

**S02** — I had spent six weeks building the forecast she was about to present.

**S03** — At my desk, I opened her slide deck on the desk display. The title said “Mira’s forecast.”

**S04** — Yesterday I had asked about credit. She said, “Let me lead this one. I’ll mention your work.”

**S05** — My own printed summary was with me at the desk. I walked to the meeting room with it.

**S06** — The director looked at the title slide and said, “Mira, this could become our board proposal.”

**S07** — Mira began showing the figures. I recognized the change I had made the previous evening. I hadn’t heard my name.

**S08** — Before questions, we had a short break. I stepped into the hallway. Mira and the director stayed in the meeting room; the door was open.

**S09** — I could return to the room and step into the hallway again during the break. When the meeting resumed, I could go back to my seat or stand nearer the director.

**S10** — I looked at my summary, with my name at the top. Below it was the revision I recognized on the screen.

**S11** — When I returned and the meeting resumed, the director asked, “Anything to add before we use this?”

**S12** — I could speak to the room then, ask the director now that we clarify the credit privately after the meeting, or let this question pass without speaking. I didn’t know whether Mira would still mention me or how she would write my recommendation if I challenged the credit.

**S13** — I was willing to let her present. I wanted my work named, and I wanted to keep the contract.

**Pre-boundary ends here. No material below this line is passed to semantic generation or player scene state.**

### Actual act — R01

I said, “I built the forecast, and I can explain the revision.” My voice shook.

### Why — R02

I could accept not being the presenter. I couldn’t accept watching my work become hers in the board proposal.

### Aftermath — R03

Mira did not look at me. After the meeting she said I had embarrassed her. I kept the contract, but she stopped inviting me to preparation meetings. A month later, the director asked me to present the next update. I still don’t know whether speaking helped my contract or nearly cost it.

### Deliberately withheld

The content of the recommendation, the director’s private view, and Mira’s motive are not disclosed. The account does not establish why the contract was retained.

Withheld is not a missing field to repair. No image, alt text, option rationale or anonymous narrator supplies it. The private account is released as an editorial first-person encounter, with its fictional label visible throughout.

## N. Text-only control — fully edited

Present the following as plain, generously spaced editorial prose with the same fictional label and no world art, walking or observation buttons. I had spent six weeks building a forecast. I was on a six-month contract, and my lead, Mira, wrote the recommendation that would decide whether I stayed. Yesterday, when I asked about credit, she said, “Let me lead this one. I’ll mention your work.”

At my desk I opened her deck. The title was “Mira’s forecast.” I carried my own printed summary into the meeting. The director looked at the title and said, “Mira, this could become our board proposal.” Mira showed the figures. I recognized the revision I had made the previous evening. I had not heard my name.

Before questions we had a break. In the hallway I looked at my summary: my name at the top, the same revision below. Mira and the director remained inside, with the door open. I could return and step out again during the break. On resuming I could sit or stand nearer the director.

The director asked, “Anything to add before we use this?” I could say I built the forecast, ask now to clarify credit privately afterward, or let the question pass. I accepted Mira presenting. I wanted my work named and wanted to keep the contract. I did not know whether she would mention me later, or what challenging her would do to the recommendation.

**Separate account:**

I said, “I built the forecast, and I can explain the revision.” My voice shook.

I could accept not being the presenter. I couldn’t accept watching my work become hers in the board proposal.

Mira did not look at me. After the meeting she said I had embarrassed her. I kept the contract, but she stopped inviting me to preparation meetings. A month later, the director asked me to present the next update. I still don’t know whether speaking helped my contract or nearly cost it.

**Account limits:** The content of the recommendation, the director’s private view, and Mira’s motive are not disclosed. The account does not establish why the contract was retained.
